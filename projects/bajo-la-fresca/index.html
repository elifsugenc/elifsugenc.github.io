PROJECT

Bajo La Fresca. /ˈbaxo la ˈfɾeska/

bajo la fresca:
Derived from a combination of 'bajo' (under) and 'a la fresca' (in the cool air), it refers to the Spanish tradition of sitting outside in the evening to relax and socialize.

TEAM Sara Serés Béjar
Özgün Ege Bilgiç
Elifsu Genç
SITE Patio San Basilio 44
Córdoba, Spain
Map / Installation Sites ↗
CONTEXT Festival Efímera
Festival: @efimera_fest ↗

“Bajo la fresca” nace de la observación de la comunidad que se genera en los patios cordobeses: espacios de encuentro y convivencia que funcionan como pequeños refugios dentro de la ciudad.

“Bajo la fresca” stems from observing the sense of community that emerges in Córdoba’s traditional courtyards: spaces for gathering and coexistence that function as small refuges within the city. The installation seeks to make visible those aspects that, although they are an essential part of the courtyard, can easily go unnoticed: the diversity of forms of life that inhabit it, the microclimate that develops within it, and the sense of shelter and coolness it provides.

Through colour, scent, and the presence of living elements, the intervention creates an experience that invites visitors to pause, observe, and feel the space.

Making a continuous landscape to solve the limited gardening space.

Our intervention aimed to mimic the gradient, colorful landscape of the existing patio (Patio San Basilio 44) while addressing the lack of horizontal gardening space. By mapping the colors of the existing flora, we designed a suspended fabric landscape.

The sensory experience of the patio was translated through a multi-layered material process. We sewed pockets into a translucent fabric and stamped it to create organic waves. Inside these pockets, we placed raw cotton that we dyed and frayed to mimic a petal-like texture.

To recreate the olfactory refuge of the patios, the cotton was infused with natural scents, including orange and lemon peels, as well as an anti-mosquito perfume, extending the functionality of the structure.

The final installation acts as a floating, sensory canopy—a modern reinterpretation of the patio's role as a shaded, cooling, and socializing refuge.
