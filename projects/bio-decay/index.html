01 CONCEPT & MANIFESTO —
“

The Space is Never Finished

The archive does not aim to preserve perfection, but to document transformation. Each layer and patch carries traces of human presence and environmental weather, making decay part of its life.

“

Microplastics to Ephemeral Matter

There are microplastics in virtually everything we eat. If synthetic plastics contaminate all food, we can invert the metabolic cycle by making biodegradable architectural skins from food waste.

“

Permanent Skeleton vs. Living Skin

A precision steel wire grid serves as an enduring framework. The biopolymer panels act as a transient skin—rotting, wrinkling, shedding, and continuously renewed through public action.

Bio-Decay began with a provocative inquiry into everyday consumption: “How many microplastics can we eat in a day?” Rather than passively enduring synthetic accumulation, the project develops an active architectural archive situated in the iconic Milli Reasürans passage in Nişantaşı, Istanbul.

The building operates as an open circular bio-refinery, harvesting organic waste—fruit peels, starch residues, coffee grounds, and vegetables—from neighboring restaurants and transforming them into functional biopolymer skins. Structured around a modular steel wire grid skeleton, the architecture rejects static permanence.

The bioplastics undergo a dynamic lifecycle of physical decay across 6+ weeks—shifting from pliable 10–30% clarity, through matte stiffening at 50%, to brittle 70–90% amber opacity and desiccation. As panels weather and fall away, researchers, workshop participants, and passage pedestrians harvest the fragments, cook new batches in underground lab stations, and re-insert fresh panels into the living frame. At its core, a stepped public amphitheatre provides a much-needed civic gathering space shaded by transforming biopolymer canopies.

Bio-Decay, gündelik tüketimin görünmeyen maddelerine dair kışkırtıcı bir soruyla başladı: “Günde ne kadar mikroplastik yiyebiliriz?” Proje, sentetik birikimi pasifçe izlemek yerine, İstanbul Nişantaşı'ndaki Milli Reasürans Çarşısı'nda yaşayan bir organik atık arşivi ve biyoplastik laboratuvarı önerir.

Yapı, çevredeki restoran ve kafelerden toplanan organik atıkları (meyve kabukları, nişasta tortuları, kahve ve sebzeler) yerinde işleyen döngüsel bir kentsel biyo-rafineri gibi çalışır. Çelik tel ızgara bir iskelet üzerine kurulan mekân, mimari kalıcılık yanılsamasını reddeder.

Üretilen biyoplastik paneller 6 haftayı aşkın bir sürede 4 aşamalı bir bozunma döngüsünden geçer: %10–30 arası şeffaf ve esnek durumdan, matlaşan ve sertleşen ara evreye, ardından %70–90 arası kehribar rengi, kırılgan ve çatlak son evreye ulaşır. Bozulan paneller toplanıp yeniden pişirilirken, ziyaretçiler ve araştırmacılar yeni paneller asarak yapım sürecini hiç bitmeyen kolektif bir eyleme dönüştürür. Merkeze yerleştirilen amfitiyatro ise Nişantaşı'nın kamusal oturma alanı eksiğine cevap vererek biyoplastik gölgelikler altında canlı bir toplanma odağı yaratır.

02 CIRCULAR METABOLISM & BIOPLASTIC CHEMISTRY —
SUGAR BASED ORANGE • CITRUS

Extracted from fruit residues and citrus waste for high initial optical transparency and radiant amber color.

  • Cleaning: Squeezing fruit residues & separating liquid sugars
  • Dilution: Diluting extracted sugars with filtered water
  • Main Station: Mixing with glycerol & natural binders
  • Stove: Controlled thermal activation
  • Molding & Drying: Pouring into wire trays & shelf curing
CELLULOSE BASED VEGGIES • FIBERS

Harvested from fibrous vegetables and plant stalks providing tensile reinforcement and textural stiffness.

  • Cleaning: Solar desiccation on open drying racks
  • Pulverizing: Grinding into micron-grade bio-powder
  • Separation: Chemical separation of plant cellulose fibers
  • Stove: Homogenizing with bio-polymers at 85°C
  • Molding: High-density flat pressing into structural skins
PROTEIN BASED DAIRY • GELATIN

Derived from dairy and protein kitchen by-products to achieve elastic flexibility and moisture sensitivity.

  • Filtration: Multi-stage membrane filtration
  • Acid Coagulation: Precision acid curdling
  • Main Station: Intensive blending and binder infusion
  • Stove: Controlled thickening under low heat
  • Casting: Viscous pouring onto catenary wire frames
STARCH BASED POTATO • RICE • CORN

Obtained from rice, potatoes, and bread scraps producing durable opaque panels with structured weathering lines.

  • Cleaning: Grating and mechanical blending of starch solids
  • Boiling: High-temperature aqueous boiling
  • Straining: Extracting pure starch slurry
  • Heating: Thermal gelatinization with vinegar and plasticizer
  • Shelf Curing: Uniform air-drying on ventilated racks

03 TEMPORAL DEGRADATION & OPACITY LIFECYCLE —
10 – 30% OPACITY
0 – 1 WEEK • NEWLY PREPARED Translucent & Pliant

Freshly cast bioplastic skin. High optical transparency, flexible tensile response, radiant surface sheen, and soft citrus aroma.

30 – 50% OPACITY
1 – 3 WEEKS • INITIAL CURING Hardening Membrane

Moisture evaporates into the passage air. Texture hardens, transparency drops, and diffuse golden amber shadows appear inside.

50 – 70% OPACITY
3 – 6 WEEKS • ACTIVE WEATHERING Matte & Micro-Fissuring

Stiffened surface loses elasticity. Deepening terracotta tone with fine micro-cracks reacting to foot traffic, humidity, and touch.

70 – 90% OPACITY
6+ WEEKS • TERMINAL OBSOLESCENCE Fragile & Organic Harvest

Nearly opaque, desiccated, and dark umber. Panels begin curling and fracturing, harvested for composting or re-cooking into new panels.
