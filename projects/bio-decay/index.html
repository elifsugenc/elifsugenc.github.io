Bio-Decay.

The Living Architecture of Ephemeral Matter

OVERVIEW An architectural archive exploring bioplastic decay,
circular material flows, and non-static spatiality.
01 / ABSTRACT & PROJECT STATEMENT

Bio-Decay reconsiders architecture not as a static permanence, but as a living, metabolizing organism.

Situated within an active urban food hub, the project transforms local organic food waste into site-specific bioplastics (sugar, cellulose, protein, and starch-based matrices). These biomaterials form the architectural skins, partition screens, and spatial volumes of the facility.

Rather than resisting environmental degradation, the structure foregrounds decay as an active, temporal design driver. As the bioplastic panels react to sunlight, ambient humidity, touch, and microbial activity, their opacity, texture, structural integrity, and color shift across their lifecycle—transitioning from newly cured opacity to translucent, worn remnants. Architecture here operates as a living archive of disappearance and renewal, documenting the intricate entanglements between human consumption, material decay, and spatial atmospheres.

02 / CORE CONCEPTUAL PILLARS 1. Circular Material Metabolism & Urban Harvesting

The building acts as an open bio-refinery fed by immediate urban flows. Organic waste collected from adjoining cafes and restaurants is sorted, crushed, and converted into biopolymer substrates on-site. Through designated waste chutes and public sorting hubs, the metabolic pipeline of the city becomes an interactive, performative civic engagement rather than hidden infrastructure.

2. Architecture as an Unfinished, Self-Rebuilding Body

The modular grid framework serves as an open scaffold. When bioplastic elements reach critical degradation or collapse, they are harvested, composted, or re-processed, while newly cured panels are inserted into the frame. The building process is never concluded; the space exists in continuous regeneration, where decay and assembly co-occur in real time.

3. Epistemic Transparency & Atmospheric Decay

The optical properties of the bioplastics function as temporal gauges:

  • 0–2 Weeks (Cured State): Rich pigmentation, deep coloration, structural stiffness, and low optical transmission (10–20% opacity).
  • 3–6 Weeks (Active Weathering): Moisture loss, micro-fissuring, surface softening, and selective light diffusion (30–50% opacity).
  • Terminal Decay (Biodegradation): Desiccation, textural dissolution, curling, and high luminous transparency (70–90% opacity), yielding dynamic shifts in shadow and daylight within the interior.
03 / SPATIAL PROGRAM & OPERATIONS

The Bio-Fabrication Lab & Sorting Level: Ground/sub-level facilities where waste triage, bioplastic casting, drying, and tensile testing take place.

The Living Archive Grid: The primary interior volume defined by high-density wire and timber scaffolds where panels age, curated by researchers and experienced by the public.

Public Forum & Stepped Amphitheater: A collective gathering zone shaded by decaying bio-canopies that modulate acoustic and visual privacy based on degradation states.

Researcher Residencies & Clean Lab: Integrated live-work quarters for material scientists, designers, and temporary collaborators engaged in long-term degradation tracking.

04 / DESIGN & RESEARCH IMPLICATIONS

By embedding biological obsolescence into spatial tectonics, Bio-Decay challenges the petroleum-derived permanence of contemporary construction.

It replaces petrochemical durability with biological stewardship, proposing a paradigm where matter is computational, ecological, and poetically transient.
