PROJECT

THE

EARTHEN.

is there death because there is life?
\n
SOFTWARE V-Ray, Rhinoceros 3D, Adobe Photoshop, Adobe Illustrator
OVERVIEW Does death exist because life exists, or does life exist because death exists?
\n
\n
01 / THE CYCLE OF EXISTENCE

Life and death are inseparable aspects of existence. Every living being experiences beginnings and endings, growth and decay, and through these processes, the natural cycles of life continue.

Death is not an ultimate end but a transformation that allows new life to emerge.

Understanding this cycle encourages us to accept impermanence, letting go of fear and attachment, and to see every moment as part of a larger continuum.

02 / URBAN CONTEXT

Maslak is dominated by business towers and plazas, filled with constant motion, deadlines, and repetition.

These structures represent the cycle of productivity and exhaustion, where life often feels mechanical and endless. The project seeks to interrupt this rhythm, offering a space to breathe, slow down, and reconnect with the body and earth.

MAKING → TOUCHING → GROUNDING → OBSERVING → RETURNING
03 / PROGRAMMATIC LOOPS

The program creates a calm, continuous loop of making, touching, grounding, observing, and returning—supporting both physical relaxation and mental renewal.

The cycle begins with Material Preparation. Clay, soil, water, and seeds are mixed through bodily gestures — pressing, walking, and kneading. The process reconnects the body with the earth, turning making into a sensory ritual where touch becomes a tool of creation.

From there, the material is molded in the Shaping & Formation Space, and placed outdoors on the Drying Terrace. Natural forces begin to alter their surface—cracking, fading, and reshaping them. The terrace becomes a living archive, documenting decay as a quiet continuation rather than an end.

04 / MATERIAL TRANSFORMATION

The sectional organization of the project is based on a vertical cycle of material transformation, moving from wet to dry, from soft to solid, and from decay to growth.

At the lowest level, water-based activities such as soaking, stepping, and mixing take place, allowing the body to engage directly with liquid and semi-liquid matter. As users move upward, the material becomes denser and more stable.

A fiberglass layer wraps around the metal pipe structure and blurs the boundary between inside and outside. The pipes descend from above and create a cave-like space, making the interior–exterior relation even more ambiguous. Some of the tubes act not only as structural supports but also as channels for seeds, water, and clay, guiding materials between levels.

05 / REBIRTH

As the organic produce in the garden undergoes natural decomposition, the seeds within begin to germinate. As the clay erodes, seeds sprout through its fragments. New life grows from what has decayed, turning the material into fertile soil.

These seeds are harvested and then collected in designated seedbins, which are connected via a tube system to the Material Creation Lab. Here, the biological matter is reintroduced into the production phase, creating a perpetual, closed-loop system.

Just as our lives carry the invisible traces of smaller cycles, the architecture embodies these accumulated layers of biological memory.
