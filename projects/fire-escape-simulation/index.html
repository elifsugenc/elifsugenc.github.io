FIRE ESCAPE SIMULATION

Project Manifesto

Agent-based computational simulation analyzing human panic dynamics, spatial bottleneck congestion, and egress optimization during high-rise fire evacuation emergencies. Interrogating how built thresholds and structural geometries dictate the political ecology of survival.

Individual Role & Contributions

Design Team Lead • 3D Modeler & Level Designer: Leading the spatial design and modeling workflow; constructing the 1:1 detailed digital model of the MEF University building complex; researching empirical fire behavior and material combustion dynamics; designing spatial egress corridors, door width thresholds, and obstacle geometries for crowd evacuation simulations.

Core Inquiries
“Is being affected by fire political?”

Alongside: “Can you escape the fire?” Evacuation is never merely a technical question of exit widths; it is a question of spatial justice. Who gets to escape, who is immobilized by architectural bottlenecks, and how do built thresholds distribute vulnerability during extreme crisis?

01

Political Ecology of Egress & Spatial Inquiry

SIMULATION INQUIRY • 2026
Disaster is never neutral. The common assumption that fire treats every body equally ignores the violent spatial politics of architecture. Egress bottlenecks, vertical circulation hierarchies, locked exit doors, flammable cladding materials, and delayed evacuation warnings are not sudden accidents—they are architectural and economic decisions enacted long before the fire begins.
Fire Escape Simulation asks:
“Is being affected by fire political?”

When an emergency breaks out, survival is unevenly distributed by spatial zoning and regulatory compromises. High-density structures and institutional campuses do not merely enclose human activities; they choreograph who reaches safety and who is trapped in choke points. This simulation deploys computational agent modeling not to justify minimal compliance codes, but to lay bare the systemic spatial vulnerability produced by architecture.

02

Research Strands: Spatial Politics of Disaster

STRAND 01

Unequal Vulnerability

Who gets to escape? Evacuation speed is fundamentally constrained by spatial zoning. When egress routes narrow or are enclosed by cost-saving barriers, the physical burden of panic falls on the most crowded and vulnerable zones of a building.

STRAND 02

Spatial Complicity & Regulations

Beyond minimum building codes. Standard fire regulations assume uniform, rational agent movement. Real panic reveals how formal compliance frequently conceals mortal congestion risks and toxic smoke paths.

STRAND 03

1:1 Computational Digital Twin

Constructing a 1:1 detailed digital twin of the MEF University campus to simulate agent velocities, obstacle collisions, and escape trajectories—turning spatial simulation into an instrument of architectural accountability.

03 CROWD PANIC & SPATIAL TRAJECTORIES

Computational behavioral model and emergency spatial simulation analyzing crowd dynamics, spatial panic, bottlenecks, and exit trajectories under extreme conditions. Testing egress thresholds and structural circulation limits through algorithmic agent simulation.
