01 / QUESTIONING THE BRIEF

The project began with a brief: the encounter of two actions.

But, wait...

What is an action? When does movement become action, and does an action require intention?

02 / THE APPROACH: DESIGNING CONDITIONS

Rather than dictating behavior through rigid spatial programming, the project explores how design can create conditions for unpredictable actions.

Drawing from Situationism, Psychogeography, and the concept of the Flâneur, STRAY questions the expected urban interior. It shifts focus from prescribing actions (walking, running, passing) to enabling deviations (stopping, wandering, meeting).

INTENTION → ACTION → DEVIATION → ENCOUNTER → REACTION → NEW ACTION

03 / INTERACTIVE SURFACES & MATERIALITY

Architecture as an active participant: transferring emotions, experiences, and senses through responsive materials.

In STRAY, boundaries are not fixed walls but moveable interfaces and fabric pages. These interactive surfaces allow the space to be shaped directly by the users, transforming passive visitors into active participants who record and leave physical traces of their encounters.

04 / PROGRAMMATIC AMBIGUITY

Spatial typologies that blur the line between movement and dwelling.

Spaces such as The Ramp, The Dark Room, and The Workshop Area act as physical catalysts. They do not tell people where to go or what to do; instead, they introduce spatial disruptions that provoke curiosity, slowing down movement to encourage spontaneous physical and social interaction.

05 / ANOTHER QUESTION

STRAY began by asking what an action is.

Instead of defining the actions that should take place, the project evolved into an exploration of how material systems and spatial ambiguity can create conditions for behaviors that cannot be predicted in advance.

This left me with another question:

Can we design an action without designing what the action will be?
