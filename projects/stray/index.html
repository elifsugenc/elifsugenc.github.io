STRAY

Concept & Premise

STRAY investigates the boundaries between purposelessness and presence in contemporary urban life. Grounded in 19th-century theories of the Flâneur, Situationist dérive (psychogeography), and the Slow City philosophy, the project questions the utilitarian tyranny of efficiency: "Is doing nothing doing something?" and "Is not doing anything even possible?"

What Is An "Action"?

If a cat is not running to an appointment, is its movement any less real? Why do we label purposeless wandering as "stray" or "laziness", while our own calendar-bound rush is seen as meaningful?

Is Doing Nothing Doing Something?

Questioning action itself: Is "doing nothing" doing something? Can doing nothing be an active state of presence? Why aren't we the ones who are stray, disconnected from sensory reality?

The Spark: Why "Stray"?
The project began by questioning a familiar term: stray animals. Why do we call them "stray"? In daily life and across internet culture, there is a beloved meme: a street cat abruptly bolts across the road at full speed, prompting people to joke, "I guess he just remembered he had important business to attend to!" But do street animals actually have business? If they don't, what are they doing? Why are they labeled "stray" for roaming freely without a fixed calendar, while we, bound to rigid routines, consider ourselves purposeful? Why are they the "stray" ones, and why aren't we?

Questioning Action & Idleness:
This curiosity opened up the core philosophical dilemma: What is an action? Is doing nothing doing something? Or is doing nothing even possible? In an efficiency-driven city, every human movement is pressured to produce utility. Yet a cat lying on a warm stone or exploring an alleyway is never in a void; it is fully present, attuned to sound, shadow, and ground. Doing nothing is not an absence of action—it is a heightened state of perception.

The Spatial Translation at Moda Seaside:
Responding to the studio brief, "Encounters at the Seaside: Intersection of Two Actions", the project stages an encounter between purposeful human routine and purposeless straying. Located behind the coastal tree canopy of Moda, Kadıköy, the building acts as a continuous ascending ramp rather than a rigid enclosure. Passersby drift inward from the coastline, leave tactile marks on rotating surfaces, engage in flexible workshop pods, and arrive at The Dark Room—a quiet sanctuary lit solely by natural daylight slits and the passing silhouettes of people outside.

Çıkış Noktası: Neden "Stray" (Başıboş)?
Proje, her gün kullandığımız yalın bir kelimeyi sorgulayarak başladı: sokak hayvanları (stray animals). Onlara neden "stray" (başıboş, rotasından sapmış) diyoruz? İnternette ve günlük hayatta sıkça karşılaştığımız tanıdık bir meme vardır: Bir sokak kedisi bir anda fırlayıp delicesine koşmaya başlar; biz de arkasından güleriz: "Herhalde önemli bir işi çıktı, bir yere yetişiyor!" Peki gerçekten bir işleri var mı? Yoksa ne yapıyorlar? Günleri takvimlerle, mesailerle ve hedeflerle sınırlı olmayan bu canlıları neden "başıboş" olarak yaftalıyoruz da, asıl amaçsız bir döngüde koşturan kendimizi öyle görmüyoruz? Neden onlar stray de biz değiliz?

Eylemi ve Eylemsizliği Sorgulamak:
Bu gözlem doğrudan projenin ana sorusuna dönüştü: Bir eylem nedir? Hiçbir şey yapmamak da bir şey yapmak mıdır? Yoksa hiçbir şey yapmamak gerçekten mümkün müdür? Günümüzün üretkenlik odaklı dünyasında amaçsızca gezinmek bir boşluk veya zaman kaybı olarak görülür. Oysa bir kedinin kayalıkta durması ya da sokakta dolaşması asla bir boşluk değildir; aksine çevresini, rüzgarı, ışığı ve dokuları tüm duyularıyla hisseden aktif bir varoluş halidir. Hiçbir şey yapmamak bir boşluk değil, mekânsal bir farkındalıktır.

Moda Sahilinde Mekânsal Karşılaşma:
Stüdyo teması olan "Encounters at the Seaside: Intersection of Two Actions (Sahilde Karşılaşmalar: İki Eylemin Kesişimi)" doğrultusunda proje, iki zıt eylemi karşı karşıya getirir: insanın hedefe kilitli günlük rutini ile amaçsızca gezinme (straying). Kadıköy Moda sahilindeki ağaç dokusunun arkasında konumlanan yapı, keskin duvarlar yerine kesintisiz yükselen bir rampa omurgası kurar. Sahilden içeri süzülen ziyaretçiler rampa boyunca yavaşlar; dönen yüzeylere izler ve çizimler bırakır, hareketli panellerle dönüşen atölyelerde vakit geçirir ve en üstte sadece sabah ışığı ve gölgelerle aydınlanan Karanlık Oda'da (The Dark Room) içsel bir dinginliğe ulaşır.

PSYCHOGEOGRAPHY

Exploring the urban landscape not just as physical matter, but as an affective, emotional terrain shaped by subjective feelings and collective memories.

SITUATIONISM

Urges urban dwellers to break from automated routine, constructing ephemeral situations that uncover hidden emotions and unexpected connections.

SLOW CITY (CITTASLOW)

Promotes a relaxed, contemplative tempo of life in the city, enabling people to notice overlooked details and foster authentic community belonging.

FACADE ELEVATION & HISTORIC CONTEXT Contextual dialogue: The contemporary intervention with perforated magenta screens, cumba/bay observation nooks, and a transparent circulation tower set against the historic masonry balconies of Moda.
03 TECTONICS, SURFACES & MOVEABLE SCREENS —
THE 4-CASE MOVEABLE WALL SYSTEM

Suspended from a modular 1/50 ceiling track system, sliding woven magenta wall panels allow the ground and workshop levels to metamorphose dynamically:

CASE 01
CONTINUOUS FLOW

Panels retracted to allow completely seamless, unobstructed circulation between the frontyard and research archive.

CASE 02
PRIVATE ALCOVES

Partitions deployed diagonally to form intimate study alcoves and quiet reading pods for solitary visitors.

CASE 03
WORKSHOP & EXHIBIT

Panels arranged perpendicular to daylight to form exhibition pin-up surfaces and collaborative workshop zones.

CASE 04
STRUCTURAL STORAGE

All 4 moveable plaques nest fully flush within the thickness of the structural column grid, completely disappearing.

ROTATING MATERIAL DISPENSER Surfaces along the ramp are wrapped weekly in rotating materials (kraft paper, raw canvas, touch-sensitive sheets, chalk coatings) allowing visitors to leave tactile traces of their presence.
THE RAMP EXPERIENCE (VIEW Q) Ascending through the vibrant magenta ramp volume; visitors stop spontaneously to draw, write ("i love scribbling!"), and engage in unscripted creative dialogues.

Dynamic double-height workshop and gathering space framed by moveable perforated wall screens, pink ceiling beams, and visual apertures connecting to the ramp.
